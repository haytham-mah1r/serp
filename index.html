Builds a Google search URL with the uule, hl, and gl parameters, so results render as if the search came from an exact point on the map. Everything runs in your browser.

For a clean, logged-out view: right-click the link above → “Open link in incognito window” (Chrome/Edge) or “Open in new private window” (Firefox), or copy the URL into a private window. Pages can’t launch incognito windows themselves — browsers block that by design.

How it works: with coordinates set, the tool encodes an a+-type uule — a standard-base64 blob of a small location record (role, producer, provenance, a timestamp, the lat/lng in E7 fixed-point, and a radius) — which pins the search to an exact point, the same way ad-preview tooling does. Without coordinates it falls back to the older w+ canonical-name encoding. To verify what Google thinks, search for where am i and check the map onebox, or scroll to the bottom of the results page where Google states the location it used.
